# Kantola five-year 2-D runs 1 to 9, calibrated CEC 13 set (Bouchet, R2025a)

Campaign tag: `Base_2D`

The 2-D half of the calibrated all-12 campaign: runs 1 to 9 of `Run_Matrix_12.m` (control, basalt and calcite on the `whole_field`, `margin_crop` and `margin_prairie` layouts, each a 2 x 2 main grid plus the 5 m margin, twelve columns with lateral transport). Runs 10 to 12, the controller, the parameter set and the provenance assertion live in `runtime/Base_1D`; read that README first.

## Parameter set

Alpha 1.3 (as the sweep formed it) with the matched logKH vector, KNmaxd 0.0048 d^-1 and Kbot free drainage (NaN) are passed as `Run_One_Subfield` options from `Campaign_Parameters.m` and apply to every column of a run; CEC 13 and the Appelo scheme come from the pinned workbook and parameter script. ## Revision 2026-09-13: four pre-resubmit fixes

The first submission (array 26157692) lost elements 7, 8 and 9 (`margin_prairie`) at model day 153 (1 June 2016, before any amendment) with `ERW:invalidCalciteLimit`; the other nine runs completed. Diagnosing that failure found three further, unrelated defects, all fixed in `tc_all12_prerun_fixes_20260913_v1`:

1. `ERW_Calcite_Reaction_Limit.m` treated a dissolved pool that rounds a few ulps below zero after inventory-limited precipitation as an invalid state (`ERW:invalidCalciteLimit`, the error the array reported). It now clamps values inside the rounding tolerance (the larger of 1e-10 and eight ulps) to the exhausted pool the arithmetic intended.
2. `ERW_function.m` floored only the left side of the log-space Appelo exchange relations. When the selectivity product predicts an exchangeable fraction below the 1e-24 floor, the residual `ln(1e-24/f_pred)` cannot be removed by any admissible fraction, and the step was refused (`ERW:NoConverge`, dominant `f_Al`) at the exact mass-conserving root. Both sides now share the floor. Above the floor `max()` returns the sum unchanged, so a step that converged before is bit-identical; the replay of the refused step and its regression test (`test_erw_appelo_exchange_floor`, fixture `erw_appelo_al_floor_step22053.mat`) are in the audit suite.
3. `PFT_Prairie_Margin.m` inherited the crop file's placeholder nutrient reserves (Careserve/Mgreserve/Sireserve seeded at 1000 g/m2 for both PFT slots) without the crop's harvest event that clears them every year (`CropHeightType.m`, the `ManI==-2` branch): the margin is an established perennial, never sown or harvested. `Nutrients_Available.m`'s uncapped relative-concentration factors then evaluate to hundreds against an actual plant nutrient content of a few g/m2, both inflating litter nutrient return without bound and saturating `Plant_Nutrient_Suppression` at 1 (uptake fully suppressed) -- a one-way nutrient pump into solution with no sink. This is the source of the >0.4 eq/m2/d unbalanced base-cation influx that put the margin_prairie column in the state that exposed defects 1 and 2 in the first place. Every reserve is now seeded at zero instead, the state the crop is in immediately after every harvest. Regression test `test_prairie_margin_reserve_seed` is in the audit suite. (A partial fix for part of this defect -- Nreserve_L/Preserve_L/Kreserve_L only -- was already present on this checkout as of 2026-09-08; this revision extends it to Careserve/Mgreserve/Sireserve on both PFT slots, which the partial fix did not cover and which is what actually drove pore-water pH past 11.)
4. `ERW_Daily_Step_2D.m` computed the daily Ca/Mg/K/Si delta handed to ERW as `P_c(j,:) - P_c(j-1,:)`, where `P_c(j-1,:)` already contains that day's own hour-1 lateral transport (`Biogeo_Lateral_Pools`, called every hour in the 2-D driver) folded in before this function first read it -- so the delta captures only what changed between two already-lateral-adjusted snapshots, and 23 of every 24 hours of lateral Ca/Mg/K/Si exchange between cells never reaches ERW's tracked chemistry (the domain-total mass stays conserved; it is a per-cell misallocation, not a mass-balance bug). PO4/NH4/NO3 do not have this problem because `ERW_Daily_Step_2D.m` already re-anchors their ERW-side totals directly to the T&C pool array every day; Ca/Mg/K/Si now do the same, using the identical `anchor_row`/`anchor_state_row` pattern. Regression test `test_erw_lateral_cation_daily_delta` is in the audit suite. This defect is confirmed too small (one to two orders of magnitude) to be the source of the margin's pH-11 excursion (defect 3 is), and affects only 2-D layouts with more than one lateral cell (runs 1-9); runs 10-12 are single-column and bit-for-bit unaffected.

Defects 1, 2 and 4 are properties of the solver and the driver that would matter in any run reaching the relevant regime; defect 3 is what put the margin_prairie columns in that regime in the first place. Installing 1, 2 and 4 without 3 would let margin_prairie run to completion while its pore-water pH stayed pinned near 11 to 13 for as long as the placeholder reserve lasted -- plausibly most or all of a 5-year run, since the seed is roughly five times the soil's entire cation exchange capacity.

After installing the package, move the interrupted result and log trees of both campaign tags aside and resubmit fresh through the controller; the new validation stamps carry all four fixed files and the updated pins. Runs 1 to 6 and 10 to 12 never invoke `PFT_Prairie_Margin.m` (crop columns only); a step that converged under the old Appelo residual is bit-identical under the new one unless its exchangeable Al fraction was below 1e-24; and the lateral-cation fix changes only how much of each day's already-conserved lateral transport reaches ERW's own tracked totals, not the transport itself -- so for runs 1 to 6, where field and margin share similar chemistry, differences from the previous partial run should be small, and for run 10 to 12 (no lateral transport) none at all. Runs 7 to 9 (margin_prairie) are expected to differ from the interrupted attempt starting day 1 -- defect 3 changes their trajectory by design -- so there is nothing to compare them against; treat the first rerun as the first real result for that layout, and watch its early days (or run a short probe first) to confirm pore-water pH now stays in a plausible range before letting the full 5-year jobs run to completion.

## Revision 2026-09-22: Davies activity coefficients in the saturation indices

One chemistry change made after the 2026-09-18 campaign completed, installed by `tc_erw_activity_coefficients_20260922_v1`. It follows from the campaign's own diagnostics: after four doses (200 t/ha) the whole-field calcite run had 183 t/ha of calcite undissolved and 12.6 t/ha re-precipitated as secondary carbonate, because the reported calcite saturation ratio sat between 1.0 and 3.1 in every month from the first application on, so the affinity term held dissolution at zero. The same calibration sweep's Kbot 0.07 and free-drainage cases (which bring soil CO2 down from 70-109 % to under 10 % of soil air) dissolve the same 17 t/ha, with pore-water Ca still 26-28 mM and Omega still 1.0-1.5: the reservoir's calcium is buffered by the 13 cmolc/kg exchanger, not by drainage, and at that ionic strength (0.05-0.1 mol/L) the concentration-basis Omega overstates the true saturation state by the product of two divalent activity coefficients, a factor of four to six. On the recorded June 2017 state (Omega 2.161, pore pH 6.13, Ca 24.2 mM) the activity-corrected ratio is 0.44.

1. `ERW_function.m` forms the pore-water ionic strength once per step from the ion list its charge balance already carries (`ERW_Ionic_Strength.m`, new; Al on its umol/L basis) and the Davies activity coefficient of the divalent ions (`ERW_Activity_Coefficient.m`, new; A = 0.509, no free parameter, the same family SCEPTER uses). Calcite and magnesite saturation (`Omega_CaCO3`, `Omega_MgCO3`), the primary calcite affinity in `ERW_Calcite_Kinetics.m`, the closed-water equilibrium bound in `ERW_Calcite_Reaction_Limit.m` (the trial state during its bisection, with the coefficient held at the step's incoming value) and the primary silicates in `ERW_Mineral_Saturation.m` all read on the activity basis. In `ERW_Mineral_Saturation.m` every reaction quotient is already the full quotient raised to 1/n_H, so substituting gamma*c for each ion is the exact activity form and no formula changes; Si (H4SiO4, neutral) takes no correction. `ERW_MOD_PARAM_excel.m` applies the same basis to the t = 0 state. The charge-balance and exchange solve keeps the concentration basis it was inherited with; that, and the Al(OH)3 target, are documented limitations left for a later revision.

2. `ERW_Mineral_Saturation.m` now takes `Al_CEC_scale` and divides Al by it. The solver carries Al on the conv_Al (nmol/L) basis and the function had been reading that value as umol/L, so albite, anorthite and zoisite saturation ratios were overstated by 1e3 to the power of their Al exponent (5.6 for the plagioclases, 4.9 for zoisite). Callers that hold Al in umol/L pass 1, the default, and reproduce the previous result.

3. All three functions default their new arguments to the inherited behaviour (ionic strength 0, activity coefficient 1, scale 1), so every existing direct call reproduces its pinned value; the two production callers pass the new arguments explicitly, and `test_erw_mineral_kinetics` and `test_calcite_primary_kinetics` assert on the source that they do. New `test_erw_activity_coefficient` checks the Davies values against hand-computed references, the ionic-strength sum, and the recorded June 2017 state.

`ERW_function.m`, `ERW_MOD_PARAM_excel.m` and `run_post_legacy_equation_audit_tests.m` take new pins; the other chemistry files and tests are covered by the stamp's general globs. `ERW_function.m` and `ERW_MOD_PARAM_excel.m` are inside `ERW_Equivalence_Fingerprint`, so the 1-D equivalence baseline must be regenerated on a `cpumodel:9575f` node with `-singleCompThread` before validation. Results change by design in every treatment that carries rock (calcite directly; basalt through its 2.6 % calcite, its ferroactinolite and the plagioclase saturation state), and in the control runs only through the secondary-carbonate saturation state; the finished 2026-09-18 result trees must be moved aside before a fresh submission. The 1-D runs 10-12 are the first comparison (a `tools/calcite_budget.m` mass balance and `tools/co2_check.m` on the new and the 2026-09-18 results); the all-12 campaign is resubmitted once the chemistry revisions are complete.

## Revision 2026-09-22b: aluminium leaching on the solver's Al scale

One two-line correction to `ERW_function.m`, installed by `tc_erw_al_leaching_scale_20260922_v1` on top of the activity-coefficient revision and kept separate from it so that its effect is attributable on its own.

The ERW solver carries dissolved aluminium on the `Al_CEC_scale` basis (`conv_Al = 1e3`) and `Al_tot` on the matching basis: the `Al_w0` seed, the `AlOH4` hydrolysis closure and the `Al_tot` balance all fix that scale. The leaching term removes the mobile species (free Al3+ and Al(OH)4-) in the drained water as `min((Al+AlOH4)*W*f_Lk, Al_tot, Lw*1000*(Al+AlOH4))`, the same construction as `Leak_Na` and `Leak_An`; the first term is the mobile inventory on `Al_tot`'s basis and reduces to `Al_tot*f_Lk` when all dissolved Al is mobile. The inherited line multiplied that first term by `1e-3`, which put it on the umol basis against the other two terms and held aluminium leaching about a thousand times below the drainage every other species sees. The factor is present at the same two lines of the original TC-SMEW code (`Other models/T&C-SMEW`, `ERW_function.m` 263 and 271), so removing it is a departure from the legacy equation; it is recorded as one in `POST_LEGACY_EQUATION_AUDIT.md` section 3a with the derivation, and `test_erw_al_leaching_scale` pins both corrected lines and replays the construction on the solver scale.

Expected effect: aluminium now leaves the reactive zone at the drained fraction instead of accumulating, which lowers `Al_tot`, the exchangeable-Al fraction and the Al(OH)3 target over the run. At this site's pH range (5.6 to 6.9) the dissolved pool is small, so the effect on pH and on the N fluxes is expected to be modest; it is measured by the 1-D control/basalt/calcite comparison against the activity-coefficient results (the reference is the rerun of 2026-09-22, not the 2026-09-18 campaign). `ERW_function.m` and `run_post_legacy_equation_audit_tests.m` take new pins; `ERW_function.m` is fingerprinted, so the 1-D equivalence baseline must be regenerated before validation, as after every deploy that touches it.

## Revision 2026-09-23: carbonate saturation on the ion activity product

A correction to the 2026-09-22 revision above, installed by `tc_erw_omega_activity_basis_20260923_v1`. That revision put the Davies coefficients on the saturation indices, which is exact for the silicates in `ERW_Mineral_Saturation.m` (each ion enters the reaction quotient as `gamma*c` directly) but not for the carbonates, because `CO3` is not a concentration. `ERW_function.m` forms `CO3 = k2*k1*CO2_w/H^2` from the thermodynamic `pk1`/`pk2` with concentrations, and `pH = -log10(H/conv_mol)` confirms `H` is a concentration; carrying activities through, `K1*K2 = a_H^2*a_CO3/a_CO2` gives `a_CO3 = CO3/gamma_mono^2`, so the stored `CO3` is the true carbonate activity times `gamma_mono^2`. The calcite ion activity product is `(gamma_div*Ca)*(CO3/gamma_mono^2)`, so the saturation index is `gamma_IAP*Ca*CO3/K_CaCO3` with `gamma_IAP = gamma_div/gamma_mono^2`. Using `gamma_div^2` assumed `CO3` was already a concentration and suppressed Omega by a further `gamma_mono^6` - 1.9x at I = 0.01, 2.3x at I = 0.02, 3.3x at I = 0.05 - an error that strengthened with ionic strength and so grew with weathering and with time.

1. `ERW_function.m` forms `gamma_mono` beside the existing `gamma_div` and passes the single factor `gamma_IAP` to `Omega_CaCO3`, `Omega_MgCO3`, `ERW_Calcite_Kinetics.m` and `ERW_Calcite_Reaction_Limit.m`; `ERW_MOD_PARAM_excel.m` applies the same to the t = 0 state so step 1 and every later step share a basis. The two calcite routines take the factor under its own name and default it to 1, which reproduces the inherited concentration basis exactly. `ERW_Mineral_Saturation.m` is unchanged - the silicate substitution was already exact.

2. The factor is written as the explicit ratio rather than as `gamma_mono^2` so that an activity model other than Davies stays correct. Under Davies `log10(gamma)` scales as `z^2` with one shared `f(I)`, so `gamma_div = gamma_mono^4` and the ratio collapses to `gamma_mono^2` exactly. New `test_erw_omega_activity_basis` pins the source at all six sites, the Davies identity across an ionic-strength grid, linearity of Omega in the factor, and the end-to-end statement that `gamma_IAP*Ca*CO3` equals `a_Ca*a_CO3` reached by an independent route.

3. Validated before packaging, in side tree `omA_20260922` off the installed checkout, runs 10 and 11 at the campaign's own calibrated parameters. The control is unchanged in every year to the last printed digit (6.051, 5.980, 5.913, 5.964, 6.002), because no Omega-gated process fires in a plot with no amendment. The basalt growing-season residuals move from -0.007, +0.301, +0.393 (2018-2020) to -0.141, +0.002, +0.103, giving RMSE 0.116 and bias -0.055 - the best absolute fit of the three chemistry states held, against 0.135 / -0.110 for the pre-activity chemistry and 0.237 / +0.089 for the 2026-09-22 revision.

`ERW_function.m`, `ERW_MOD_PARAM_excel.m` and `run_post_legacy_equation_audit_tests.m` take new pins; the two calcite routines and the new test are covered by the stamp's general globs. `ERW_function.m` and `ERW_MOD_PARAM_excel.m` are inside `ERW_Equivalence_Fingerprint`, so the 1-D equivalence baseline must be regenerated on a `cpumodel:9575f` node with `-singleCompThread` before validation. Results change by design in every treatment that carries rock and are unchanged in the controls; the 2026-09-22 result trees must be moved aside before a fresh submission. Not a legacy departure: the legacy chemistry carried no activity coefficients at all. The speciation itself remains on the concentration basis with thermodynamic constants, so `Alk` and `DIC` are still low by `gamma_mono^2` and `gamma_mono^2*gamma_div`; correcting that moves the charge balance in every run including the control and invalidates the alpha / KNmaxd / Kbot calibration, and is recorded in `POST_LEGACY_EQUATION_AUDIT.md` section 3b as a separate decision.

## Revision 2026-09-23b: soil pH measured on the field study's 1:2.5 extraction

The bulk-pH operator compares the model against a laboratory measurement, so it has to reproduce that measurement's water:soil ratio. Beerling et al. (2023 preprint, arXiv 2307.05343), Soil analyses: "Soil pH was measured in 1:2.5 soil-to-water paste solution ... to an aliquot of 10 g air-dried soil (sieved <2mm) 25 ml of ultrapure water was added in a 50 ml Falcon tube. Samples were mixed by vortexing at maximum speed for 10s. pH measurements were obtained to the third decimal place". The production rows of the parameter workbook carried `pH_DilutionFactor` = 2, a 1:2 extraction; they now carry 2.5. `n_extract` is read from that column alone and reaches both the forward operator (`Porewater_to_Bulk_pH.m`) and the initialisation inverse (`Invert_Bulk_pH.m`, called once from `ERW_MOD_PARAM_excel.m`), so field-to-extract and observed-to-field use one assumption. `test_pH_conversion` asserts the production rows carry 2.5.

The 2024 PNAS version cites its pH method by reference only (Carter & Gregorich, *Soil Sampling and Methods of Analysis*); its own "10 g ... 25 mL" figure belongs to the sodium-phosphate Si extraction at fixed pH 6.0/6.5/7.0, which is a different assay. The preprint is the source for the pH ratio.

The workbook was edited surgically at the XML level so that exactly five cells change (`pH_DilutionFactor`, rows 2-6 of the production sheet). A library rewrite of the same file was rejected: it additionally materialised `rho_rock` into row 3, which had been deliberately blank, and that would have changed a production density silently.

Two protocol details were recorded here when the ratio was corrected, and both have since been acted on. Air drying removes the CO2 the field pore water carried, so the transfer of field DIC into a gas-tight extract was an assumption; since package u the paste stands open to the laboratory air and its dissolved CO2 is the air's (`PASTE_OPEN_TO_AIR_2026-10-02.md`; the `CO2_air_extract` argument now sets it). A ten-second vortex followed by an immediate reading is far from equilibrium, which argued for keeping residual solid calcite out of the extract; since package q the plot's solid carbonates dissolve in the paste to saturation instead (`PASTE_CALCITE_2026-10-01.md`), because a soil holding free carbonate does not read its diluted pore water. The operator therefore gives the equilibrium the reading tends to, with the air and with the carbonates, not the state ten seconds of mixing reaches; for a soil without solid carbonate the two conventions differ by about 0.01 or less in the August mean, and by up to 0.1 on single days.

This revision changes bulk pH in every treatment including the control, so the 2026-09-22 and 2026-09-23 result trees must be moved aside and the campaign resubmitted. `ERW_MOD_PARAM_excel.m` is inside `ERW_Equivalence_Fingerprint`, so the 1-D equivalence baseline must be regenerated before validation.
